# [HASH] — [selection-formatting-spans] created and cleared: bold, italic and underline applied to a selection from the row above the keys, after the platform documentation turned "no keyboard does this" into a position rather than an obstacle

Session of 2026-09-18, 15:42.

Captured by the user mid-session and processed in the same exchange, on the process-now branch. His complaint in his own framing: most places support bold, underline and italic, and yet no keyboard seems to have them anywhere — so formatting something means copying it out into somewhere that can style it, editing it there, and pasting it back.

**The research is what made this designable, and it went in two directions.** The standing answer to why no keyboard offers this — in Google's own support threads and in how the platform is described — is that styling belongs to the app and the field rather than to the keyboard. That is a position, not a limitation: `InputConnection.getSelectedText()` with `GET_TEXT_WITH_STYLES` returns the selection as a `SpannableString`, and `commitText` takes an ordinary `CharSequence`, documented as "the composing text with styles if necessary" and pointing at `Spanned`. So read-toggle-commit is a supported round trip. Read from an archived copy of the official SDK reference, `developer.android.com` having returned only its navigation index; the finding and that caveat are in `workshop/resources/research/keyboard-applied-text-styling.md`.

**The user's own workaround turned out to be the evidence the design rests on, which is the part worth keeping.** Nobody here can test which apps preserve styling — there is no `adb` on this machine and no route to the handset. But his workaround ends by pasting the styled text *back* into the app he took it from, which is the same path a commit takes, so that app keeps styled text. That is one app's worth of evidence and the item says so rather than claiming a survey.

**Two questions narrowed it before anything was designed.** Asked whether he wanted the selection case or a toggle armed before typing, he said selection — which removed the armed-toggle design and its interaction with the composing text the correction engine manipulates. Asked where he hits the problem, he said absolutely everywhere, which is what produced the split: styling reaches the plain Android fields, markers reach the messaging apps, and the two are separate work. He was offered the choice of designing both together and took the recommendation to split — a recommendation Claude failed to give in the turn that asked, and supplied at the next one.

**It spends nothing, because the row's middle is shared by moment rather than divided.** [completion-slots] holds it while a word is in progress, [tap-word-alternatives] when a finished word is tapped, [predictive-saved-words] when a correction has just been undone. A non-empty selection is a fourth moment and none of the other three can be live during it; the discriminator against [tap-word-alternatives] specifically is that one firing on a cursor landing inside a word and this one on a selection that is not empty. That ordering was written into all four entries.

The failure mode is stated in the item rather than left to be discovered: where the field will not keep styling, the commit goes through, the styling is dropped and the characters are unchanged — a control that appears to do nothing rather than one that damages text. Web-based editors are the expected case, and a live ProseMirror issue records Gboard and rich web editors already disagreeing about spans inside a word.

SPEC gained a sentence at the decision step, this being the first thing Hexboard does that changes text rather than offering to. It sits on the right side of SPEC's own line — the user selects and presses, so nothing is judged on their behalf — and the sentence was widened later the same session when [selection-formatting-markers] made "alters no character of it" untrue.
