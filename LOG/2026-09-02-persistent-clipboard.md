# 37384e4 — The clipboard screen given a route in, a shape and a deletion gesture, all three settled by the user in one exchange

Everything about what the clipboard *holds* was settled on 2026-09-01 — the hour-or-twenty retention rule, the five-minute class for password-field and source-marked-sensitive clips, no clear-all, the flag cleared by informed consent. What kept this in Unprocessed was that there was no clipboard screen to add to and no settled panel structure to reach it from. Both of those changed during this session.

How it is reached: a button at the left end of the row above the keys, which [suggestion-strip] builds and which the microphone already occupies the right end of. It spends nothing — no gesture, no key slot, no vertical space beyond what the row already costs — where every other route spends something, and it is where Gboard puts its own clipboard entry, so it is the familiar place to look. Two routes were ruled out: a swipe is unavailable, horizontal swipe having been spent on panel switching by [panel-switch-gestures] that morning; and reaching it the way emoji are reached does not exist, a grep of the Kotlin having found no vertical swipe and no emoji panel anywhere — which became [emoji-panels].

What it replaces: the board area only, with the row staying put and the button toggling in and out. The reasoning is that the keyboard's total height never changes, so nothing under the thumb jumps when the list opens over the very text field being pasted into; the way back is the control you came in by; and it keeps the clipboard inside the keyboard rather than sending the user to an app screen, which would mean leaving the text field — the failure this project keeps designing against. An app-screen clipboard and a taller overlay both lost on that. The cost accepted: the board area is four rows tall, so three or four clips are visible and the rest are scrolled, which is the same trade Gboard makes.

Two interactions completed it. Tapping a clip pastes it and returns to the keys — one action for the thing the list was opened for, which rules out selecting a clip without pasting and rules out multi-paste, neither of which was wanted. And the bin appears across the bottom of the board area while a drag lasts and vanishes with it, the bottom edge being where a thumb pulls something it wants rid of and the top being where the next clip arrives from as the list scrolls; releasing anywhere but on the bin returns the clip, so an aborted drag is the default rather than something to aim for. A permanently visible bin lost twice: it spends space in an area already showing three or four clips, and it can take an accidental tap that a drag-only target cannot.

The privacy flag stays cleared on the footing recorded on 2026-09-01 — informed consent to a persistent store, with four protections and two stated limits — and nothing this session changed that footing.
