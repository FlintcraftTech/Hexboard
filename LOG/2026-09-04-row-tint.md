# [HASH] — Rows given alternating resting fills, so the zigzag still reads as QWERTY rows

Written 2026-09-04 at 15:57.

Raised by the user on 2026-09-02, and his reason is the wedge itself: the rows are still there, they are only zigzagging, and people read left to right — so the row is the unit that carries recognition, and QWERTYUIOP is a string almost everyone knows on sight. Highlighting rows says *this is the keyboard you already use*; highlighting columns would say nothing, because nobody reads a keyboard downwards. What this defends is SPEC's familiarity principle: the zigzag is the one thing that makes Hexboard look unfamiliar, and this is what tells a new user it is not.

Tinting the keys rather than banding behind them was also his, the same day: a band drawn behind a zigzagging row is not a stripe but a wave, and would be the first non-circular shape on the board. Tinting the circles adds no shape at all. His words for how far to take it were nothing crazy, just enough to notice.

How far is derived rather than picked. Each key was already drawn as `lerp(fill, lit, glow)` — resting at one colour, fully lit when pressed — so even rows rest at the existing fill and odd rows one-fifth of the way along that same line. The tint therefore introduces no new colour, cannot clash with the theme, and its distance from a pressed key is measurable rather than a matter of taste: a press travels the whole way, so a pressed key stays four-fifths brighter than its unpressed neighbours even in a tinted row. That is what keeps this clear of [key-press-feedback], which shipped on 2026-09-02 and makes the pressed key's lightening the only press feedback there is. The one-fifth itself is Claude's proposal and is derived from nothing — it is the smallest step expected to read as deliberate rather than as a rendering artefact, and it is one constant, adjustable once seen.

The item also carried a second, unrelated instruction, and it is worth recording why it travelled here: `nearestCentre`'s comment gained *why* full coverage matters rather than only that it holds. A tap that lands nowhere is a deletion, and the neighbour-weighted correction planned in [uniform-neighbours-predictive] can repair a substitution but not a character never entered — so dead space between circles would produce the one failure that engine cannot fix. It went into this item because this was the work already going to that file.

Refused: a band behind each row (a wave-shaped non-circular element); tinting by column (carries no recognition); a stepped tint one shade per row (the difference accumulates down the board instead of reading as banding); and a hand-picked shade (clashes with the theme and makes the distance from the press colour a matter of taste).
