# [HASH] — [emoji-panel-reach] answered with a browser costing one emoji slot, after one of its three feared costs turned out not to exist

Five panels of fifty reach 250 of Unicode 16.0's 3,781 emoji; the other 3,531 are in the bundled file and on no panel. Not a defect in the build, which did what its item said and used the arrangement its reference names — but a user wanting the 251st has no route at all.

The item had recorded three costs, and the first dissolved. It said scrolling panels would break the fixed-height promise the clipboard screen was designed around; they would not, because [persistent-clipboard] settled exactly this shape on 2026-09-02 — the board area is replaced by a scrolling list while the keyboard's total height never changes, and the same control returns to the keys. The mechanism this needed had already been designed for another feature.

So the answer costs one of the 250 slots: the panels stay as they are, and one slot opens a browser over the whole list in Unicode's own groups. The other two feared costs stay refused — more than five panels spends a swipe, and a search field spends space in a row that already has the microphone, the clipboard button and suggestions claiming it.

Worth stating because it looks otherwise: none of this is governed by the manifest rules. SPEC puts the emoji panels outside their scope deliberately, so spending a slot on a control evicts nothing under any rule.

Held against [persistent-clipboard] rather than for tidiness — two independent implementations of the same board-area swap is how a keyboard ends up with two slightly different ways of covering its own keys.
