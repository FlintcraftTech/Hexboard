# PLACEHOLDER — [language-starter-layouts] rewritten whole a second time: eleven-column Russian rows transcribed from FlorisBoard's layout file, with Ё and Ъ the only hidden letters and improvisation ruled out

Planning record for [language-starter-layouts], 2026-09-02 (afternoon). The morning's record of the same slug carries the first rewrite; this one supersedes it.

**The premise the morning's design rested on was wrong by four.** It counted 30 letter slots in the QWERTY panel's three letter rows; four of them are backspace, cursor-left, cursor-right and enter, so English fills 26 exactly and Russian's 32 letters would have needed six hidden behind long-presses, not two. Confirmed against `resources/key-layout.json` in this session. The finding had halted the /next run and was filed as [qwerty-letter-slot-count].

**Three routes were offered and all three lost to the user's rule: do not improvise.** Six invented pairings compounded a judgment already awaiting a Russian reader; dropping the cursor keys to make 28 slots was another invention; accepting that some alphabets do not fit broke SPEC's promise. The user supplied a screenshot of Gboard's Russian keyboard, which settled it: the standard mobile Russian layout is eleven columns wide (rows of 11, 11 and 9), with Ъ and Ё the two letters hidden. There is no ten-column standard to copy, so the morning's rejection of widening — Claude's reasoning, on the big-keys thesis — lost to copying the standard, on Russian alone. The tenth-narrower key is the trade every Russian typist already takes on Gboard.

**Placement of the function keys, settled with the user:** all four rows eleven wide, because the Kotlin sizes keys from the panel's widest row; backspace at the right of row 2 where Gboard has it; enter at the right of row 3 where Gboard has it; cursor-left in the one spare slot; cursor-right dropped, since Gboard has no cursor keys and there is no eleventh slot — named on the item as a decision, not a loss. The user's condition, in their words: "as long as we don't have missing keys, that's the main thing." The item's landing observation makes that checkable: the manifest lists all 33 Russian letters, 31 as keys and two as long-press entries.

**The source moved from the user's screenshots to a file, within the same hour.** The user proposed screenshotting Gboard for every language, then found Gboard offers five Russian layouts and said the visual reading was not work they should be doing. Claude proposed the open-source keyboards' layout data and, on the user's go, searched with them present: FlorisBoard's `jcuken_russian.json` carries the identical 11-11-9 rows and its `ru.json` popup file puts ё under е and ъ under ь and nothing else, all under Apache 2.0. AOSP LatinIME carries the same data behind per-locale indirections and is a cross-check; the GPL forks were avoided on licence. Filed as `workshop/resources/research/open-source-layout-sources.md`, and the earlier `cyrillic-overflow-and-slot-budget.md` carries a superseded line naming what falls and what stands. SPEC's layout principle now names open-source layout data as the source and says rows widen rather than letters being hidden.

The item is cleared to run, with a `README.md` attribution line added to its build. The Russian reader's check, [russian-layout-check], stays held behind it and is now a confirmation of a copy rather than a judgment on Claude's pairings.
