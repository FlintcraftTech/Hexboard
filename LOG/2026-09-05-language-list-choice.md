# [HASH] — [language-list-choice] deleted after the plan it was waiting to make was settled, the user's question about dictionary size having reshaped it

The item asked which languages get a layout and in what order. Half was already in SPEC — added as they are asked for — and the user asked whether that policy was "yet another thing that wasn't changed in SPEC when we made the change to accepting keyboard libraries instead of crowdsourcing". Checked against the 2026-09-02 record: it was written *as part of* that change, not inherited from before it. His instinct was pointing one change later than he thought, at the cost model this session had itself removed.

The decisive contribution was his: does a language not come with its whole dictionary, and should languages be downloaded separately? An earlier answer here had measured only the layout — 17 KB against a 640 KB emoji file already bundled — and concluded a separate download was pure overhead. That was incomplete, and saying so mattered: a language you can type *well* in also needs a word list, which is a megabyte or two, and SCOWL is English-only so every other language needs its own with its own licence to read. The Hunspell set is the multilingual answer and its per-language dictionaries are separate works whose licences its own documentation says to go and check. So "adding a language is cheap" holds for the 17 KB half and fails for the megabyte half.

The plan that came out of it is two-speed: layouts ship in the app, all of them; dictionaries arrive separately and on demand; a language therefore arrives in two steps, and a layout with no dictionary is still usable because only the correction is missing. Play Asset Delivery makes the second half possible without Hexboard ever making a network request, which is the property that let it be accepted at all. Layouts are ordered by what the config model can express rather than by speaker numbers, and Chinese, Japanese and Korean are out of scope entirely because they need candidate conversion rather than a key inventory.

The item was deleted because every part of it landed somewhere: the plan into SPEC, the batch into [first-batch-layouts], the delivery into [dictionary-asset-packs], the two-standards question into [competing-layout-standards], and the cost research into its own file. The FlorisBoard catalogue and the four-tier model would have gone with it and were rescued by the session's rescan into `open-source-layout-sources.md`.
